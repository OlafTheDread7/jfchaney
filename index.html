The Story

The long way here

Some people go straight from A to B. I took the scenic route — and it taught me more than any straight line ever could.

There was a stretch of my life where things came apart. I'm not going to dress it up: I made mistakes, I lost ground, and I had to do the slow, unglamorous work of rebuilding — one honest day at a time. What I learned in those years is the thing I'm proudest of. You can change. It's hard, it's daily, and it's absolutely possible.

That season gave me the tools I use in everything now: showing up when it's uncomfortable, being honest about where things actually stand, asking for help before problems get big, and keeping promises small enough to keep. It turns out those are the same skills that make someone good at managing projects — and good at earning back trust.

Today I get to put that to work. I manage IT projects for a living, I taught myself to code, and I started a business helping local Richmond companies get found online. I still show up early, still write things down, and still believe the best thing you can build is a reputation for doing what you said you'd do.
